# What a Fantasy League Knows About Markets

Financial markets hide most of what you want to measure. Ownership is reported
quarterly, if at all. Intent is invisible. Fantasy Premier League has the opposite
problem: **everything** is observable — every manager's holdings, every transfer, every
week, across a £7.5B ecosystem.

## The Behavioral Ownership Gap

If you can compute what optimal ownership *should* be and observe what it actually
*is*, the difference is a clean measurement of collective behavioural error. I called
it the Behavioral Ownership Gap and built a Fama–MacBeth panel across **380 gameweeks**.
The t-statistic came back at **−57.20** — not a subtle effect.

## Putting a number on it

The gap is not free. Modelled as a welfare cost, it runs to **£137.1M a year**, and it
is attributable to information architecture rather than to individual foolishness: the
platform makes some facts easy to see and others expensive.

## Out of sample, or it didn't happen

The obvious critique of any behavioural finding is that it was fitted after the fact.
So the validation model was **pre-registered and frozen** before testing, then run as an
equity-style long–short replication. It returned **105 profitable weeks out of 105**.

Under review at the *Journal of Sports Economics*.
