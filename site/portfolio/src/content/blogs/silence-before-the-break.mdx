# Silence Before the Break

Attention is supposed to move markets. More eyes, more trades, more volatility — that
is the intuition, and for most of the range it holds. The question I kept circling was
what happens at the top of that range, when a market is already saturated with
attention and there is nothing left to notice.

## The identification problem

Attention and volatility cause each other. A volatile market draws eyes; eyes make a
market volatile. Any naive regression is estimating a circle. I used **Lewbel
identification** — heteroskedasticity-based instruments constructed from the data
itself — to break the loop without needing an external instrument that does not exist
for something as diffuse as investor attention.

## The threshold

Rather than assuming where the relationship bends, I estimated it. Profile likelihood
recovers a structural threshold of **γ\* = 1.5625**, with a Hansen confidence set of
[1.14, 2.50]. Every standard error is Driscoll–Kraay, because 22 markets in the same
week are anything but independent.

## The Silence Signature

Past that threshold, volatility does not accelerate. It *falls*. Markets go quiet
exactly when everyone is watching — and that quiet precedes the breaks. I called the
effect the Silence Signature, and it survives synthetic-control falsification in **14
of the 22 markets** individually, not just in the pooled panel.

## Corroboration from a different direction

An empirical threshold is only as convincing as its robustness, so I rebuilt the
question from scratch as an agent-based simulation — nine behaviourally distinct trader
archetypes, 10,200 Monte Carlo runs — and estimated the same parameter by Simulated
Method of Moments. It returned γ\* = 1.5625 again, from a completely different
methodology.

The paper is peer-reviewed at *Borsa Istanbul Review*.
